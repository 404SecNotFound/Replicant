## What the detection must establish

Catch one host resolving many distinct, algorithmic-looking domains that mostly do
not exist. A single NXDOMAIN is noise. Hundreds of distinct, never-repeated
second-level names failing from one host inside an hour is a generator searching
for its rendezvous domain.

## Why the obvious approaches miss it

- **Any NXDOMAIN at all.** Normal hosts produce a steady trickle of NXDOMAIN from
  typos, stale records and protocol probing. A rule that alerts on any NXDOMAIN is
  useless in production, and this technique emits a benign trickle so that a rule
  like that scores badly here too.
- **Many subdomains under one parent.** That is REP-004, DNS tunnelling. Here each
  name is a different second-level label, so a per-parent volume rule sees one
  query per parent.
- **Blocklists.** The names are generated fresh each epoch, so no list holds them.

## Data model

Held across the run are `src` (the host), `dst` (the resolver) and `dpt` (53).
Varying per record are the queried name, the response code, `rt` and the
transaction id.

|---|---|---|---|
| queried name | `FTNTFGTqname` | `PanOSDNSQuery` | `destinationDnsDomain` |

## Detection logic (SIEM-neutral pseudocode)

```
window          := 1h
min_nx_names    := 50       # distinct NXDOMAIN second-level names per host
min_nx_ratio    := 0.8      # NXDOMAIN share of this host's DGA-shaped answers
min_mean_label_entropy := 2.8   # bits per character, mean over the labels

for each src over the window, responses where rcode in (NXDOMAIN, NOERROR):
    names  := distinct second_level_label(qname) where rcode = NXDOMAIN
    if len(names) < min_nx_names:                       continue
    ratio  := count(rcode = NXDOMAIN) / count(all responses in this group)
    h      := mean(shannon_entropy(label) for label in names)
    if ratio >= min_nx_ratio and h >= min_mean_label_entropy:
        alert(src, len(names), ratio, h)
```

Entropy is taken per label and then averaged. Labels are 6 to 20 characters, so a
single label's entropy is capped by its length. Averaging over many labels is what
separates the generator from the trickle.

## Setting the thresholds

Measured over 20 seeds at each preset. Every attack record comes from one host,
and the run spans about one hour whatever the preset.

| Preset | names per epoch x epochs | NXDOMAIN share (catalog `nx_ratio`) | label length | attack mean label entropy (lowest seed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 50 x 4 = 200 | 0.95 (measured 0.94, the integer rounding of 47 of 50) | 8 to 12 | 3.02 |
| medium | 200 x 6 = 1200 | 0.97 | 7 to 16 | 3.15 |
| high | 800 x 8 = 6400 | 0.99 | 6 to 20 | 3.25 |

The benign trickle is 12 NXDOMAIN responses per run from the same host, at every
preset and seed, alternating the labels `wpad` and `isatap` under one parent. Its
mean label entropy is 2.13 on every seed. `min_nx_names = 50` is a quarter of the
smallest attack and four times the trickle. `min_mean_label_entropy = 2.8` sits
between 2.13 and 3.02.

Every attack name is a second-level label directly under the reserved `.invalid`
TLD. No generated name can resolve, and that must not be relaxed.

## False positives and tuning

`[Inference]` from general practice, not measured here. Real NXDOMAIN bursts come
from misconfigured search domains, software probing (WPAD, ISATAP, which the
trickle imitates), browsers' random-label captive-portal checks, and security tools
resolving many names. The random-label browser checks are the hardest, because
they also look algorithmic. Allowlisting those agents and requiring the burst to
repeat across epochs are the usual mitigations.

still holds the whole run.

## What a green result proves, and does not

REP-016 is **parser-only**. A green result shows the rule counts distinct failing
names per host, computes a failure share and a lexical score, and stays quiet on a
small typo-and-probe trickle. It does not exercise any stage keyed on a realistic
TLD, on domain reputation, or on registration data, because every name sits under
`.invalid`.

It does **not** prove end-to-end delivery. Every timing and delivery claim in this
project is loopback-only until the first observed rule fire in a real SIEM.

## Review notes for the owner

- **Judgment calls to confirm.** The three thresholds above are mine, placed
  between measured attack and trickle values. The contract names NXDOMAIN ratio
  and label cluster shape as the separating features. The spec uses both, and adds
  the distinct-name count.
- **Finding on the benign trickle.** It is 12 records at every preset, and it
  never includes a successful lookup, so its NXDOMAIN share is 1.0, higher than the
  attack's. A real host's NXDOMAIN share is low because most of its lookups
  succeed. The ratio clause in the pseudocode does not separate attack from
  trickle here; the name count and entropy do. Whether the trickle should carry
  successful lookups and scale with the preset is an engine question, not part of
  this spec. The 2026-10-07 catalog review treated a count ceiling that does not
  scale with the preset as a separable feature elsewhere.
